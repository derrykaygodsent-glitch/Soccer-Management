# Touchline World: from a dream to a football career

## The promise

The first question is not “Which team do you manage?” It is “Where do you live, what do you have, and who will take your call?” The world already plays football before you arrive. Football stays the main activity: a life choice changes who you can train, how you can prepare or where you can work. It never decides a match score for you.

This is a design for a new mode, not a replacement for the playable Riverside season. All places, people, clubs, brands and public offices are fictional. Start the new world from a clean **content pack**. Do not delete the current game, player accounts or anyone’s private saves.

## What exists before the manager arrives

Start small enough for every league to matter. Proposed first map:

| Country | Football pyramid | One club with a past |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ovela | First League → Valley League → district qualifiers | Loombridge Athletic began as a mill workers’ side. Its members still own the ground. Fans remember a title-winning youth captain and protest when academy players are sold. |
| Nerava | Harbour Premier → Coast Division → port qualifiers | Lantern Quay was founded by ferry crews. A flood forced it to share a rival’s pitch. Its sponsor wants naming rights to pay for the return home. |
| Istren | Crown League → Highland Division → town qualifiers | Cairn Bell won two cups before its benefactor left. Its board now insists on a wage cap, even during promotion fights. |

For the first full pack: two divisions of eight clubs per country, a domestic knockout cup and a regional tournament for champions. In a division each club plays every other club twice. A win earns three points, a draw one. Break ties by goal difference, then goals scored; use a playoff if promotion or relegation still depends on a tie. One club goes up and one goes down between divisions. District qualifiers decide entry to the bottom division against its last club. Publish these rules and dates before the season. Never insert a new team halfway through a league table.

Every club gets a founding year, founders, ground and lease or title, colours, crest, shirt, local rival, ownership rules, debts, wage budget, youth facilities, supporters and dated milestones. Seed several completed seasons using the same results that generate the honours list. Start the manager during the next season, with real results already in the table. Simulate the rest even if the manager is unemployed.

Every player gets a stable ID, face, name, nationality, age, positions, skills, current ability, potential range, fitness, injury history, wage, contract and personality. Give staff, agents, directors, sponsors and reporters names, motives, relationships and memories. Scouts see estimates, not hidden ratings. Each year academies produce new people ('newgens') with fresh profiles and assets; older players retire, and some become staff. Pay prize money from a published competition budget. Record medals, club honours, top scorer and player, youth and manager awards against the season in which they were earned.

## The first playable journey

1. **Create a person and a save.** Choose name, appearance, pronouns, home country and background. A broke local coach starts with time and trust but little cash. A former scout knows people abroad. A wealthy founder can fund travel and rent facilities but cannot buy a league place or loyalty.
2. **Earn the first chance.** Give each week limited time for paid work, scouting, coaching and home. Apply for jobs; acceptance depends on openings, skills and references. Offer casual shifts with no application barrier as a recovery route. Keep personal cash separate from the club account. Putting your savings into the club is a recorded loan or investment, not free money.
3. **Find footballers.** Visit local matches, hold trials and ask previous coaches for references. Travel abroad costs money and time. A trusted contact may host a visit or send a report without paying for a trip. A wealthy stranger still needs a player’s consent, a free registration or a transfer agreement, and wages they can afford.
4. **Turn a group into a club.** Pick a name and kit, recruit a coach, negotiate a sponsor and agree the club charter with directors. A sponsor promises a stated fee for stated duties; a director may offer accounting skills or a pitch contact without buying any shares. Show precisely what each person gives and receives before signing.
5. **Register and qualify.** Proposed district licence: 15 eligible players including two keepers, a head coach, a home pitch agreement, safety approval and enough money for the entry fee. The founder may be the coach. Show missing requirements, deadlines and the next application window. A shared or leased pitch is enough. A rejected club can still play friendlies and try again.
6. **Play.** Pick the XI, set tactics, brief the team, kick off manually and coach the existing Classic 2D match. Feed its actual goals, cards, injuries and result back into the world. An away qualifier against a rival should matter as much as a later title final.
7. **Build for the next year.** Renew leases or buy land, purchase an existing ground, or build in stages. Show planning time, safety checks, construction costs, debt payments, upkeep, capacity and ticket income before committing. Bad crowds can make renting wiser than ownership.

## Founder, owner, director and manager are different jobs

| Role | What it means | What can change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder | You started this club. | Never erased from its history, even if it folds. |
| Owner or member | You hold shares or membership rights under its charter. | Sales, new investment or insolvency can change those rights. |
| Director | You have a seat and vote on the board. | Election, appointment, removal and term limits follow the charter. |
| Manager | You select and coach the team under a contract. | You can resign, be fired or accept another club’s offer. |

**Example:** You own 60 of 100 voting shares. An investor asks the club to issue 50 new shares. If you approve and buy none, you still have 60 shares but now control only 40% of the votes. Show that calculation before the choice. Under a charter where shareholders elect directors and directors appoint the manager, a new majority can replace the board; that board can dismiss you from the job. It cannot simply take your shares. In a supporter-owned club the founder may never have held a majority at all.

Other clubs approach for sporting results, youth development, style and reputation. Before accepting, compare salary, length, authority, family move and the cost of ending the current contract. You can leave the dugout and keep shares, dividends and founder status. Under the proposed integrity rules, a manager joining a direct rival must step away from sensitive decisions at the old club; controlling both clubs in the same competition is not allowed. Offer a sale, a genuine transfer of control where permitted, or the choice to decline the job.

Board conflicts should come from recorded promises, finances and personalities, not a surprise dice roll. A broken contract, invalid vote or withheld wages can go to mediation or a fictional sports tribunal. Show evidence, cost, time and possible outcomes: back pay, a new vote, compensation or losing the case. Losing a fair vote is not grounds to win the club back in court.

## Life around the pitch

- **Players:** negotiate minutes, wages and moves. Captains, mentors, friends and rivals influence morale. A private conversation, training plan or promise kept can change a relationship over months.
- **Home:** optional partners, marriage, children, caring responsibilities, illness and recovery. Moving abroad may help a career but disrupt a family. Let the manager take leave or hire an assistant; family is not a compulsory bonus system.
- **Public life:** reporters have beats and sources. Press conferences create quotes that appear in news, radio, fan accounts and social discussions. Fans also react at the ground. Separate confirmed news from rumours. Long service, charity work or a national-team win could earn an invitation from a fictional president. Knowing powerful people opens doors, not guaranteed results.
- **Betting:** fictional odds and fan reactions can colour a match. A stoppage-time equaliser may delight the home end and ruin a fan’s prediction. Losing bettors do not speak for all supporters. No real-money bets, purchases or cash-outs. Club insiders cannot bet on their competition; approaches to fix a game can be refused and reported.
- **More stories:** a ground-share dispute during cup week; an academy graduate returning as coach; a sponsor objecting to the sale of a captain; an old rival recommending the unemployed founder for a school coaching job. Trigger stories only when their facts are true. Let characters remember what happened without repeating the same interview every week.

## Failure, recovery and time

A failed licence, bankruptcy, dismissal or lost tribunal ends a chapter, not the save. Always leave a low-cost next action: take casual work, coach a community session, help a scout, earn a reference, then apply for a job or found another club. Former clubs keep playing or leave a historical record if dissolved. Success brings new responsibilities rather than an end screen. Seasons roll over; contracts expire, clubs rise and fall, generations retire. Voluntary retirement or, if enabled, serious illness and death can end a person's career. Offer a successor in the same world, or preserve it as a career archive.

## Saves and mobile

Each new career gets its own world seed, content version, calendar, manager ID and private save slot. Starting Career B must not advance Career A. Provide named manual saves and rotating autosaves at safe points: after a signed deal, a processed day or a match checkpoint. Show last saved time and whether it is local or synced. Keep offline play and optional private cloud handoff; if devices disagree, offer a new copy instead of silently overwriting progress. Existing Riverside slots use a different format.

Design the phone screen first: one **Next action**, a short list of today's obligations, a visible money/time cost before choices, touch-friendly scouting and team selection, and a readable pitch in landscape. Never make a fast tap or constant internet connection a condition of keeping a job.

## Build order and proof that it works

1. **World and save foundation:** separate Story Mode entry, fictional content pack, stable IDs, seeded calendar and career slots. Prove two careers progress independently, a table agrees with its results, promotion creates a valid next season and 30 simulated years produce no duplicate fixtures or missing champions. Run distant games cheaply; keep detailed play for managed matches. Store annual summaries instead of every simulated frame.
2. **Founder to first whistle:** ship one country first. One casual job, one trial, one sponsor deal, a pitch lease, registration and one qualifier using the real match engine. Test a poor start with no contacts, a rich start, rejection and recovery, manual kickoff and a phone reload halfway through the match. This is the first playable milestone, not a map full of empty menus.
3. **Club control and careers:** shares, board votes, contracts, outside offers, disputes, courts and insolvency. Test a manager being dismissed while retaining shares, and a founder who leaves but later returns.
4. **Living world:** add other countries, travel, academies, personalities, relationships, stadium projects, media, betting reactions, civic honours and succession. Test that money and results are recorded once, old news cannot describe future events, and no one signs a retired player.
5. **Long-term release gate:** simulate several generations, measure save growth and restore time on a modest phone, verify privacy and revision conflicts, and play a poor founder's career through failure and a second chance. Add future story packs by version rather than rewriting old worlds.
