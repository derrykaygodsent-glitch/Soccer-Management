# Connected football roadmap
1. Contracts and transfers. First playable slice: career-local professional terms, agent replies, weekly payroll, expiry and renewal. Next: selling clubs, fees, country windows, loan returns and rival recruitment budgets. Keep founding catalogue immutable; historical matches must not change after a transfer.
2. Dated promises: record parties, evidence, review date and successor obligations.
3. League pyramid: promotion, relegation, entry, scheduling and financial consequences.
4. Grounds, ownership and sustainable club investment.
5. Succession: maintain obligations, history and family identities through job changes.
6. Player rivalries and a living football press (planned):
   - Build rivalries from repeated close title, scoring or individual award races and decisive meetings. Use permanent player IDs; no preselected stars. Distinguish a sporting comparison from personal hostility.
   - Keep a dated rivalry record: first comparison, lead changes, decisive matches, moves, international encounters, reunions, decline and retirement. Preserve the record across clubs, leagues and seasons.
   - Every significant rivalry milestone produces a public story. Quiet weeks favour other football rather than invented quotes or duplicate headlines. Allow local rivalries to grow into national and international coverage as the stakes rise.
   - Fill the wider feed from actual results, upsets, records, transfers, academy breakthroughs and board announcements. Mix match previews, illustrated comparison panels, post-match reactions and retirement retrospectives; link to players and competitions. Label fan opinions separately from confirmed news; keep private career mail private.
   - Acceptance: simulate multiple nations for ten seasons; verify stats against archived results, deduplicate stories on reload, follow a pair after transfers, and preserve a final comparison after retirement. Test seasons without a standout pair too.
