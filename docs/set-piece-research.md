# Research behind the v0.4.8 set-piece overhaul

## Throw-ins: hands, range and field position

- [IFAB Law 15](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-throw-in/) specifies the exit spot, both hands from behind and over the head, feet on or outside the line, and a two-metre opponent distance. The ball is in play when it enters the field. A throw-in cannot score directly. [Law 11](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/offside/) exempts direct receipts, not a subsequent pass.
- [Stone, Smith & Barry (2021)](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/27964/) tracked 16,154 Premier League throw-ins. First contact (83%) and retaining possession (54%) were different outcomes. Backward and lateral throws generally did better than long forward ones. [Casal et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0294317) studied 2,658 La Liga throw-ins; speed of the restart, pressure, zone and the distance and direction of the throw all mattered. These findings inform choices, not fixed outcome targets.
- [Linthorne & Everett (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763140608522877) measured release speed, height and angle during maximum-effort throws by one player. It is useful for calibrating a projectile model, not for asserting a maximum for all players.

**In the game:** The thrower gets to the line and plants before lifting the ball with both hands. A dedicated projectile starts at hand height. Accuracy, throwing power and fatigue constrain the release. A spare outlet behind the thrower often beats a marked one upfield; an open runner or a strong thrower near the opponent's box can justify going longer. With no outlet the player throws into nearby space. There is no path from a throw-in to a generic kicked clearance. The aim range (up to about 30 metres for a strong thrower) is a model parameter, not a Law of the Game or a cap on how far a bouncing ball can travel.

## Free kicks, discipline and penalties

- [IFAB Law 13](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/free-kicks/) distinguishes quick kicks from managed distance, and a direct kick from an indirect one. Defenders are normally at least 9.15 metres away; attackers must be at least one metre from a wall of three or more. The law does not require a wall for every foul. [Law 14](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-penalty-kick/) specifies a kick from the mark, with the goalkeeper on the line and the remaining players behind the mark, outside the area and at least 9.15 metres away.
- IFAB guidance on [positioning and movement](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/guidelines/positioning-movement-and-teamwork/) and [signals and the whistle](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/guidelines/body-language-communication-and-whistle/) distinguishes decisions, distance management, and the signal to restart. [Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/) distinguishes careless, reckless and serious fouls, and in general requires discipline to be administered before restarting.
- [Valkanidis et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0243287) found in a virtual-reality study that a wall blocking the initial view could delay a keeper's reaction. That downside is separate from how often the wall actually stops the ball.

**In the game:** A nearby taker can identify and use a quick outlet. Otherwise, the location and angle inform the risk of a shot or delivery. Appropriate defenders assemble a wall that covers one side of the goal; the keeper covers the other. The kick waits for the actual positions, referee and taker, rather than executing after a timer alone. A shot can hit the wall or beat it; the keeper can see around the wall or lose sight of the release. The official moves to incidents, signals the ruling, shows cards near the offender, and signals managed restarts. The taker's set-piece skill, composure, fatigue and situation affect execution; the keeper and the ball still determine the result. Not every possible infringement, delay countdown or advantage situation is simulated.

## Football Manager as a presentation and decision reference

The [public FM23 manual](https://community.sports-interactive.com/sigames-manual/football-manager-2023/playing-a-match-r4736/) describes changing the amount of footage separately from speed and replays, so a manager can observe how their instructions play out. The [FM24 set-piece feature notes](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/set-pieces-refresh-and-coaches-debut), a separate later release, describe attribute-based assignments, different throw-in intentions by pitch zone, and an optional wall based on need. These are design references. This implementation uses Touchline's own attributes, pitch geometry and ball model. No Football Manager files or private engine logic were used.

**In the game:** Key, Extended and Comprehensive reserve the same minimum buildup to important incidents before cutting. Dangerous free kicks, corner awards, booked fouls and penalties qualify at the decision, not at a later goal or shot. If selected, a restart holds the edit through the approach, release, and immediate aftermath. Broader modes add other passages around that same core. A clip changes neither the engine nor its random draws.
